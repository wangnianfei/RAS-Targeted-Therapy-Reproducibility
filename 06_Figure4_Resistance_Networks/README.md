# Figure 4 — Acquired resistance to KRAS G12C inhibitors and matching strategies

A schematic written by the authors as scalable vector graphics (SVG) code with AI assistance (no AI image generation was used) and checked against the cited sources; it is not regenerated by a script in this repository. Centre: RTK–SHP2–SOS1–KRAS–RAF–MEK–ERK pathway with the PI3K–AKT–mTOR branch. Right: resistance alterations reported in 38 patients treated with adagrasib (Awad et al., *N Engl J Med* 2021) — MET amplification, oncogenic fusions and NF1 loss of function (upstream reactivation); switch-II pocket mutations (R68S, H95D/Q/R, Y96C), second-site activating mutations and *KRAS* G12C amplification; NRAS, BRAF and MAP2K1 mutations, BRAF/RAF1 fusions and PTEN loss of function (downstream or parallel bypass); squamous transformation. Adaptive EGFR feedback in colorectal cancer is from Amodio et al. (*Cancer Discov* 2020). Left: strategies discussed in Section 5.3 of the manuscript.

Files: `Fig4.svg` (source) and `Fig4.pdf` (rendered with `rsvg-convert -f pdf -o Fig4.pdf Fig4.svg`). The on-target strategy reads "RAS(ON) inhibition (alone or combined)" because, in a *KRAS* G12C-amplified model, only combined G12C-selective and multi-selective RAS(ON) inhibition caused regression (Solanki et al., *Cancer Res* 2026).
