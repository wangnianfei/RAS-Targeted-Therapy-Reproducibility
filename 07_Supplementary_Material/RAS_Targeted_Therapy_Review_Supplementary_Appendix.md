**Supplementary Methods and Results \| Registered clinical trials of directly RAS-targeted agents (main-text Section 5.1)**

We queried ClinicalTrials.gov (search cut-off date: 18 November 2026) for interventional oncology trials that name a *RAS* gene or a RAS-directed agent and retrieved 1,182 records first posted between 2000 and 2026. Each record was classified by its relation to RAS with explicit rules, and the classification was then checked record by record, in two rounds, against the registered intervention descriptions and, where these were silent, sponsor or NCI Drug Dictionary descriptions. This review removed 31 records that were not oncology trials in patients with cancer (23 pharmacology studies in healthy volunteers or participants with organ impairment and 8 non-oncology studies, including studies of the renin–angiotensin system retrieved through the abbreviation RAS) and corrected the classification of 80 trials; all decisions and their reasons are listed in the study repository. A trial was counted as directly RAS-targeted only if at least one registered intervention acts on the RAS protein or its transcript: allele-selective, multi-selective or pan-RAS inhibitors and degraders, RAS-directed conjugates, RAS small interfering RNA, and vaccines, T-cell receptor therapies or T-cell engagers directed against mutant RAS. Trials of inhibitors acting upstream of RAS (SHP2, SOS1 or farnesyltransferase) or downstream of it (RAF, MEK or ERK) were not counted as RAS-targeted unless a direct RAS inhibitor was also registered, and neither were 17 trials of multi-antigen vaccine regimens in which a RAS antigen was one of several components and patients were not selected for a *RAS* mutation. Of the remaining 1,151 trials, 355 (30.8%) test a directly RAS-targeted agent, 129 test inhibitors of RAS regulators such as SHP2, SOS1 or farnesyltransferase, 605 select or stratify patients by *RAS* status without a RAS-targeted agent, and 62 only mention RAS. The analyses below refer to the 353 directly RAS-targeted trials in patients with solid tumours; the two vaccine trials in patients with haematological malignancies are excluded, and no directly RAS-targeted trial enrolled patients with both solid and haematological malignancies. Of these, 136 (38.5%) are phase 1, 88 (24.9%) phase 1/2, 86 (24.4%) phase 2 and 43 (12.2%) phase 2/3 or 3 (Supplementary Figure S2a). KRAS G12C inhibitors are the most common drug class (201 trials), followed by KRAS G12D inhibitors (60), RAS vaccines, T-cell receptor therapies or T-cell engagers (40) and multi-RAS(ON) or pan-RAS inhibitors (39) (Supplementary Figure S2b). The United States hosts at least one site in 198 trials (56.1%) and mainland China in 152 (43.1%); 94 and 110 trials, respectively, are conducted entirely within these two countries. By region, 202 trials have sites in North America, 188 in East Asia, 93 in Europe and 80 elsewhere, and 119 trials (33.7%) are multinational (Supplementary Figure S2c). A trial may list several tumour types: histology-agnostic advanced solid tumours are listed in 153 trials (43.3%), non-small cell lung cancer in 151 (42.8%), pancreatic cancer in 104 (29.5%) and colorectal cancer in 82 (23.2%).

Registration activity has shifted towards direct RAS inhibitors since the switch-II pocket was described in 2013 \[5\]. Directly RAS-targeted trials in solid tumours accounted for 11 of 484 RAS-related records (2.3%) first posted before 2018, 107 of 210 (51.0%) in 2021–2023 and 212 of 313 (67.7%) in 2024–2026; 319 of the 353 directly RAS-targeted trials (90.4%) were first posted in 2021 or later. Most of these trials (242; 68.6%) register more than one intervention. Because registered interventions include control-arm agents, this proportion overstates the number of trials testing combination regimens.

Industry is the lead sponsor of 241 of the 353 directly RAS-targeted trials (68.3%) and of all 43 phase 2/3 or 3 trials. Academic centres, cooperative groups and government bodies lead the remaining 112 trials (31.7%), of which 54 are phase 2 or later and 71 test a KRAS G12C inhibitor.
