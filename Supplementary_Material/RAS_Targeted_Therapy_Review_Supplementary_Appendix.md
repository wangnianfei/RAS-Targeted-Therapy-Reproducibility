## Supplementary Table S3 | Mechanistic matrix of acquired resistance patterns, functional pathways, and rational combination counterstrategies in direct RAS-targeted therapy

| Resistance Mechanism Class | Specific Molecular Alteration | Affected Biochemical Pathway | Clinical Frequency (NSCLC / CRC) | Preclinical & Clinical Combination Strategy | Representative Investigational Regimen |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| On-target switch-II pocket secondary mutations | KRAS Y96C/D, H95D/Q/R, R68S, A59S/T | Steric disruption of inhibitor binding; altered switch-II hydrophobic cavity topology | 15%–25% (higher in CRC under dual vertical pressure) | Switch to multi-RAS(ON) inhibitors, pan-KRAS non-covalent inhibitors, or KRAS-directed PROTAC degraders | Daraxonrasib (RMC-6236), FMC-376, ASP3082 |
| Upstream RTK feedback reactivation & amplification | MET focal amplification, HER2 amplification, EGFR hyperactivation | ERK feedback dissolution restoring upstream GEF loading; parallel PI3K/AKT signaling | 10%–15% (MET); 5%–8% (HER2/EGFR) | Direct RAS inhibitor plus selective RTK tyrosine kinase inhibitor or bispecific antibody | Sotorasib + tepotinib/capmatinib; adagrasib + trastuzumab-deruxtecan |
| Signaling hub & scaffolding node hyperactivation | PTPN11 (SHP2) activation, SOS1 upregulation | Sustained SOS1-mediated GTP loading onto wild-type and mutant RAS isoforms | Up to 20% functional upregulation across solid tumours | Dual horizontal blockade: RAS inhibitor plus allosteric SHP2 or SOS1 inhibitor | Sotorasib + TNO155 / JAB-3068; divarasib + BI 1701963 |
| Downstream MAPK pathway bypass mutations | BRAF V600E / non-V600, NRAS Q61K, MAP2K1 (MEK1) K57N / Q56P | Autonomous downstream MAPK flux independent of upstream RAS G-domain inhibition | 8%–12% in paired tissue and ctDNA progression cohorts | Combination with downstream paradox-breaking RAF dimer inhibitors or selective MEK/ERK inhibitors | Adagrasib + encorafenib; sotorasib + avutometinib (MEK/RAF clamp) |
| Parallel survival pathway bypass activation | PIK3CA oncogenic mutations, PTEN genomic loss | Constitutive PI3K-AKT-mTOR metabolic and anti-apoptotic survival signalling | 5%–10% in refractory cohorts | Direct RAS inhibitor plus alpha-selective PI3K inhibitor or pan-AKT inhibitor | Sotorasib + alpelisib; adagrasib + capivasertib |
| Epigenetic & metabolic intrinsic resistance | STK11 loss (STING silencing), KEAP1 loss (NRF2/SLC7A11 antioxidant hyperactivation) | Immune-cold microenvironment devoid of cytotoxic T cells; resistance to oxidative stress and ferroptosis | STK11: 20%–30%; KEAP1: 15%–20% in KRAS-mutant NSCLC | Next-generation 2nd-gen inhibitors plus chemotherapy, anti-PD-1, or glutaminase/xCT-targeting agents | Divarasib/olomorasib + chemo-immunotherapy; telaglenastat combinations |
| Non-genetic lineage plasticity & histologic shift | Neuroendocrine differentiation (small-cell transformation), Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) | Complete loss of luminal epithelial identity and loss of biological addiction to KRAS oncogene | 3%–5% under prolonged covalent G12C selective pressure (frequently with TP53/RB1 co-loss) | Switch to platinum-etoposide chemotherapy; epigenetic reprogramming with EZH2 or LSD1 inhibitors | Carboplatin + etoposide + atezolizumab; tazemetostat |
