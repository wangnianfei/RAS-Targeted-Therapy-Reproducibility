**COVER LETTER TO THE EDITOR-IN-CHIEF**

*Journal: ESMO Open \| Date: 20 November 2026*

Dear Editor-in-Chief and Members of the Editorial Board,

On behalf of all authors, I am pleased to submit our Review Article entitled "Targeting RAS in solid tumours: from molecular switch dynamics and resistance networks to randomised clinical trial landscapes" for consideration for publication in ESMO Open.

Over the past two years, direct RAS inhibition has moved from early-phase trials to phase III evidence across several tumour types. Earlier reviews were written before most of these randomised data were available and focused mainly on pretreated non-small cell lung cancer. Since then, the multi-RAS(ON) inhibitor daraxonrasib has roughly doubled median overall survival compared with chemotherapy in previously treated pancreatic cancer, divarasib has improved overall survival over first-generation KRAS G12C inhibitors in non-small cell lung cancer, and adagrasib plus cetuximab failed to improve survival over chemotherapy in colorectal cancer, after which its US accelerated approval was withdrawn at the sponsor's request.

Beyond summarising trial endpoints, the review compares the designs and results of seven phase III randomised trials and discusses why gains in progression-free survival translated into an overall survival benefit in some trials but not in others, including the roles of crossover and subsequent therapy, and how future trials can pre-specify crossover adjustment. It also relates the trial outcomes to mechanism (EGFR feedback in colorectal cancer, *STK11* and *KEAP1* co-mutations, and the acquired resistance alterations documented in patients) and points out where published data are easily over-interpreted.

Finally, we classified 1,182 ClinicalTrials.gov records and, after record-level review, identified 353 trials of directly RAS-targeted agents in solid tumours; the data and code are in a public repository. The review closes with the priorities that follow from this evidence: inhibitors for non-G12C alleles, first-line and perioperative trials, and combinations matched to resistance mechanisms.
